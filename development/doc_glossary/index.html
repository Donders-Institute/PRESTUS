Glossary¶

This page defines the terms the PRESTUS documentation uses, grouped by topic; for the overall workflow see Concepts.

On this page:

  • Transducer and Equipment: the device and its driving system, reference points on the bowl, focal depth, and calibration.
  • Intensity and Safety Metrics: free-water and in-situ intensity, MI, temperature rise, CEM43, and the ITRUSST levels.
  • Head Model: segmentation, media and their properties, pseudo-CT, placement modes, and coordinate spaces.
  • Simulation: solvers, grid and sampling, the sonication protocol, and where a run executes.
  • Files and Runs: study config, output affix, cache, and study manifest.

Transducer and Equipment¶

Transducer
The device that emits the ultrasound. PRESTUS models focused, bowl-shaped transducers.
Annular array
A transducer made of concentric rings. Changing the phase of each ring moves the focus along the beam axis (deeper or shallower), but not sideways. Most TUS transducers are annular. See Transducer Definition.
Matrix array
A transducer made of many small elements that can steer the focus in 3D.
Driving system
The electronics that drive the transducer (e.g. Sonic Concepts TPO, IGT). The same transducer behaves slightly differently with different driving systems, so calibrations are per transducer and driving-system pair.
Serial, combo
The identifiers of a transducer (transducer.serial) and its driving system (transducer.combo.ds_serial) in the shipped transducer library. Setting them fills in geometry, phases, and drive.
Bowl apex
The centre of the back of the transducer bowl. PRESTUS positions the transducer by this point (transducer.trans_pos).
Exit plane
The plane of the transducer's front face, where the ultrasound leaves the device. Focal depths are usually given from here.
Geometric focus
The natural focus of the bowl, at its centre of curvature, without any phase steering.
Focal depth setting (transducer.focal_distance_ep)
The depth, from the exit plane, that the device is set to focus at. For annular arrays it selects the ring phases.
Target depth
The distance from the exit plane to the target you planned. Ideally it matches the focal depth setting; if not, the simulated focus lands at the setting, not at the target.
FLHM
Focal Length at Half Maximum: the stretch of the beam axis where the intensity is above half its peak. Manufacturers quote focal depths as the centre of this stretch. See Calibration.
Calibration
Fitting the phases and drive of the simulated transducer so that its field in water matches measurements (hydrophone profiles) of the real device. See Calibration.

Intensity and Safety Metrics¶

Free water
A simulation in water only, without a head. Measurements and manufacturer specifications refer to free water.
ISPPA
Spatial-peak pulse-average intensity (W/cm²): the highest intensity in the field, averaged over the duration of a pulse. PRESTUS reports it in free water and in each tissue (e.g. Isppa_brain).
Target ISPPA (transducer.target_isppa_wcm2, default 30 W/cm²)
The free-water ISPPA you want to deliver. PRESTUS scales the simulated head field so that the same drive would give this ISPPA in free water (ISPPA targeting).
Free-water baseline
A short free-water simulation of the same transducer that PRESTUS runs to know what ISPPA the simulated drive gives. It is the reference for that scaling.
In situ
In the tissue, after the skull. The in-situ intensity at the target is usually much lower than the free-water value, mainly because of the skull.
Ipa, Ita
Pulse-average and time-average intensity at a location. Ita includes the off-time of the protocol (the duty cycle). See Outputs.
MI
Mechanical index, a measure of the risk of cavitation (peak negative pressure in MPa divided by the square root of the frequency in MHz). MI_tc is the transcranial MI inside the skull.
Temperature rise
The increase over the starting temperature (37 °C by default) during the protocol.
CEM43
Cumulative equivalent minutes at 43 °C: a thermal dose that combines how hot tissue got and for how long. See Thermal.
ITRUSST
The International Transcranial Ultrasonic Stimulation Safety and Standards consortium. Its consensus documents define the non-significant-risk (NSR) levels the PRESTUS report compares against (e.g. MI ≤ 1.9, temperature rise ≤ 2 °C). See Reading your results.

Head Model¶

Segmentation
Dividing the MRI into tissue types. PRESTUS uses SimNIBS charm.
m2m folder
The SimNIBS output folder of a subject (m2m_sub-XXX, under path.seg). PRESTUS reads it but never writes into it.
Medium
What the ultrasound travels through: water (free water), layered (a head from the segmentation, with layers such as skin, skull, and brain), or phantom (a designed test object). See Medium Setup.
Medium properties
Sound speed, density, attenuation, and thermal properties of each tissue (medium_properties).
Pseudo-CT
An image of skull density estimated from a special MRI (UTE/PETRA). It gives each skull voxel its own properties instead of one value per layer. See pseudo-CT.
Placement mode (placement.mode)
How the transducer position and target are obtained: typed in (manual), given in MNI coordinates (mni), searched automatically (heuristic, plantus, tus_entry), or read from neuronavigation (localite). See Placement.
Voxel, RAS, MNI
Three ways to give a position: as an index into the T1 image (voxel), in millimetres in the participant's scanner space (RAS), or in millimetres in a standard brain template (MNI). See Coordinate systems.

Simulation¶

k-Wave
The open-source acoustic simulation toolbox that PRESTUS uses by default. BabelBrain and jwave are alternatives (Backends).
Grid
The 3D box of points on which the simulation is computed. grid.resolution_mm is the spacing between points: finer grids are more accurate but slower and need more memory.
PPW
Points per wavelength: how finely the grid samples the ultrasound wave. PRESTUS warns below 6.
PML
Perfectly matched layer: an absorbing border around the grid that stops waves from reflecting off its edges.
Axisymmetric
A 2D simulation that assumes the setup is rotationally symmetric around the beam axis. Much faster, for free water and simple tests.
Acoustic FOV
An optional smaller sub-grid around the beam for the acoustic simulation, to save time and memory (Partial FOV).
Sonication protocol (timing.*)
How the ultrasound is switched on and off: pulse duration (PD), pulse repetition interval (PRI), pulse train duration (PTD), and its repetitions (PTRI, PTRD). Needed for thermal simulations.
code_type
Which k-Wave implementation runs the simulation: MATLAB or C++, on CPU or GPU. A GPU makes head simulations much faster.
Platform (platform)
Where the run happens: in your MATLAB session (matlab) or as a job on a cluster (slurm, qsub). See Running on a cluster.

Files and Runs¶

Study config
Your YAML file with the settings that differ from the defaults (Configuring a study).
Output affix (io.output_affix)
A label added to output file names, to keep several runs of the same subject apart (it appears as _desc-<label>).
Cache
Intermediate results (cache/ in the output folder) that PRESTUS reuses to skip work on re-runs.
Study manifest
A table with one row per run (subject, condition, and per-run settings) for running many subjects at once (Group analysis).
